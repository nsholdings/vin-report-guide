Before you travel to buy a car

Last checked September 20, 2026

A plane ticket or a full day of driving is a cost you pay before you have seen the car. The aim of this page is simple: make sure that when you arrive, the only thing left to find out is how the car drives.

Confirm the car is real and is the car described

  • Get the VIN and confirm it is 17 valid characters — letters I, O and Q are never used. See how to decode a VIN.
  • Ask for a photo of the dashboard VIN plate through the windshield, with a note showing today's date in the frame
  • Decode the VIN with the NHTSA VIN Decoder and check the year, make, model and engine match the listing
  • Do a live video call walking around the car

If the VIN and the listing disagree on something basic, stop. See verifying an online listing.

Run every check you can from home

  1. NHTSA Recalls — open recalls, free
  2. NICB VINCheck — theft, salvage and flood reports, free, five lookups per 24 hours per IP address
  3. A full history report — title brands across states, odometer readings, liens, auction records

Pull the full record before you book the ticket. It costs less than the trip and rules out more cars.

Get an inspection done before you go

Pay for an independent pre-purchase inspection in the seller's city, arranged by you. If the car fails, you have spent the inspection fee instead of the trip. See what a pre-purchase inspection covers.

Settle the paperwork in advance

  • Confirm the seller's name is on the title and the title is in their possession — not "coming from the bank"
  • If there is a lien, agree in writing how the payoff will be handled. See liens.
  • Ask your state what you will need to register a car bought elsewhere. See buying out of state.
  • Work out how you will legally drive it home — temporary tags or transit permits depend on the states involved
  • Arrange insurance so it is in force before you drive away

Agree the price and terms in writing

Get the agreed price, what is included, and the condition in writing before you travel. It does not bind anyone to a sale, but it removes the situation where the price rises after you have already flown in.

No deposits to people you haven't verifiedA request for a deposit to "hold" the car before you have seen it or confirmed the seller is the most common setup for remote car fraud.

Plan for walking away

Decide in advance what would make you leave without the car, and plan your return trip so that leaving is possible. A buyer who has a non-refundable one-way ticket and no way home without the car has lost most of their negotiating position before arriving.
