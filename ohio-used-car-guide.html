Buying a used car in Ohio: rebuilt salvage titles and the Highway Patrol inspection

Last checked October 4, 2026

Unlike some states, Ohio does not limit salvage vehicles to licensed businesses, so the rebuilt car you are looking at may have been repaired in someone's garage. Before it could be retitled, though, it had to pass an Ohio State Highway Patrol inspection that leaves a paper trail you can ask about.

Anyone can buy an Ohio salvage car

Ohio Revised Code section 4505.11 says the insurer assigns a salvage certificate of title to a salvage dealer or any other person. That makes home rebuilds possible. It is not necessarily a problem, but it does mean the quality of the repair varies widely from car to car.

The salvage certificate carries a notice that the vehicle is a "salvage motor vehicle". Ohio law defines a salvage motor vehicle as any vehicle in a wrecked, dismantled or worn-out condition, or unfit for operation as a motor vehicle.

The Highway Patrol inspection

The Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles states that before a rebuilt salvage vehicle can be titled in Ohio, an inspection must be completed by the Ohio State Highway Patrol. Section 4505.11 says the inspection includes establishing proof of ownership and inspecting the motor number and vehicle identification number, along with documentation of the materials used to restore the vehicle.

The inspection gateway run by Ohio's Department of Public Safety lists what the owner must bring, including all of the original receipts for any parts that were replaced. Receipts for used parts must show the VIN of the donor vehicle, and receipts from casual sales by non-licensed sellers must be notarized.

Ask for the receiptsWhoever rebuilt the car had to gather receipts for the replaced parts, with donor VINs for used parts. A seller who can show them gives you a list of what was replaced. A seller who can't is asking you to trust the repair blind.

The inspection is about ownership and identity. It does not tell you whether the structural work was done well, so book an independent pre-purchase inspection.

"REBUILT SALVAGE" stays on the title

Under section 4505.11, a restored vehicle's title must bear the words "REBUILT SALVAGE" in black boldface letters on its face, and so must every later title or duplicate. If a car's history shows an Ohio salvage event but the current title is clean, find out why before you go any further — see title brands and title washing.

Ohio also has a “FOR DESTRUCTION” salvage certificate for certain low-value inoperable vehicles; those are meant for salvage dealers and scrap processors, not for return to the road.

An Ohio salvage history should show in national title data. Check the VIN before you view the car.

Odometer notations on Ohio titles

Section 4505.06 tells the county clerk to add a phrase after the mileage on the title when it applies: “exceeds mechanical limits” or “nonactual: warning - odometer discrepancy”. Either phrase means the number on the dashboard is not a reliable measure of the car's use. Under section 4549.46, failing to give true and complete odometer disclosures is a felony. See odometer rollback.

E-Check in northeast Ohio

Ohio EPA lists seven counties in the E-Check emissions program: Cuyahoga, Geauga, Lake, Lorain, Medina, Portage and Summit. Its FAQ says that if the seller gives you an unexpired passing E-Check certificate, you can use it to register the car without testing again; certificates are good for 365 days. Otherwise the car must be tested before you register it. More on what test records show in emissions inspection history.

Ohio checklist

  1. Read the title for "REBUILT SALVAGE".
  2. Ask for the parts receipts used for the Highway Patrol inspection.
  3. Check the mileage line for "exceeds mechanical limits" or "nonactual".
  4. In an E-Check county, ask for a current passing certificate.
  5. Run a VIN check and get an independent inspection.
