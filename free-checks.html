What you can check for free, and what you can't

Last checked September 6, 2026 against each operator's own documentation

Three free or low-cost sources cover different slices of a car's history. None of them covers all of it. Knowing exactly where each one stops is the difference between a useful check and false confidence.

1. NHTSA recall lookup — free

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration lets you enter a VIN and see open safety recalls. It is free and takes seconds.

What it shows: unrepaired safety recalls only.

What it does not show, per NHTSA's own documentation:

  • Recalls that have already been repaired
  • Safety recalls more than 15 years old, except where a manufacturer offers longer coverage
  • Recalls by small manufacturers, including some ultra-luxury and specialty vehicles
  • International vehicles
  • Manufacturer campaigns that are not safety-related

So a clean result means "no open recall on record", not "this car was never recalled". Source: NHTSA Recalls.

2. NICB VINCheck — free, five lookups a day

The National Insurance Crime Bureau runs a free VIN lookup built from records supplied by participating insurance companies.

What it shows: whether the vehicle has been reported stolen and not recovered, and whether it has been reported as a salvage or flood-damaged vehicle.

Limits, per NICB:

  • A maximum of five searches per 24 hours, per IP address
  • Only insurance records appear — police records are not included
  • Coverage is drawn from participating insurers, representing 92.49% of earned insurance premium in the US

A car damaged badly but never claimed on insurance can pass this check cleanly. Source: NICB VINCheck.

3. NMVTIS — the federal title database

The National Motor Vehicle Title Information System is operated by the Bureau of Justice Assistance, part of the US Department of Justice. It exists to protect consumers from fraud and unsafe vehicles, and to keep stolen vehicles from being resold.

Consumers cannot query it directly for free. Reports are sold through approved providers, and the consumer site links out to them. Source: vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov.

The gap the free checks leave

What you want to knowCovered free?
Open safety recallYes — NHTSA
Reported stolen, not recoveredYes — NICB
Insurance-reported salvage or floodYes — NICB
Title brand history across statesNo
Odometer readings over timeNo
Accident records not claimed on insuranceNo
Auction and past sale recordsNo
Service historyNo
Liens against the vehicleNo
Prior commercial or fleet useNo

The bottom half of that table is where the money is. An odometer that reads 78,000 miles today and read 140,000 at an inspection three years ago is only visible if someone has both readings. A lien you do not discover before paying becomes your problem.

A paid history report covers the second half of that table — title records across states, odometer entries, accident and theft history, liens, auction records and prior commercial use.
