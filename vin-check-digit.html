The VIN check digit: how to tell if a VIN is valid

Last checked September 20, 2026

The ninth character of a US VIN is not part of the vehicle description. It is a checksum calculated from the other sixteen, defined in 49 CFR Part 565 (VIN requirements). Its purpose, in the regulation's words, is "to verify the accuracy of the transcription" of the VIN. It lets you catch typos — and some fabricated numbers — without any database.

How the calculation works

Step 1 — Turn every character into a number

Digits keep their value. Letters are assigned values by the regulation:

LettersValues
A B C D E F G H1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
J K L M N P R1 2 3 4 5 7 9
S T U V W X Y Z2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9

There are no values for I, O or Q, because those letters are never allowed in a VIN.

Step 2 — Multiply each by its position weight

Position1234567891011121314151617
Weight876543210–98765432

Position 9 is the check digit itself, so it is not included.

Step 3 — Add the products and divide by 11

The regulation: "Add the resulting products and divide the total by 11."

Step 4 — The remainder is the check digit

A remainder of 0 to 9 is the check digit. A remainder of 10 is written as the letter X. The regulation's own worked example totals 411, which leaves a remainder of 4, so its check digit is 4.

What a failed check means

If the calculated value does not match position 9:

  • Most often, a character was mistyped — re-read the VIN from the car
  • Sometimes, the VIN was invented or altered by someone who did not know about the checksum

Either way, do not research that VIN further until you have read it correctly from the vehicle yourself.

A passing check is not a clean bill of healthA cloned VIN — copied from a real, legitimate car — passes the check digit perfectly, because it is a real VIN. The checksum catches errors and crude fakes, not copies. See VIN cloning.

The shortcut

You rarely need to do this by hand. The NHTSA VIN Decoder will decode a VIN and flag problems with it. Knowing the method lets you understand what the tool is telling you — and spot-check a VIN on your phone at a seller's driveway.

A valid check digit tells you the VIN is well-formed. The record tells you whether the car behind it is what it claims to be.
