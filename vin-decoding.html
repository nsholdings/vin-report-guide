How to decode a VIN, position by position

Last checked September 20, 2026

The 17 characters of a VIN are not random. Their structure is set by federal regulation, 49 CFR Part 565 (VIN requirements), and knowing it lets you catch a fake or mistyped VIN before you spend anything checking it.

The basic rules

  • "Each VIN shall consist of seventeen (17) characters."
  • Each character must be a digit or one of the letters A–Z except I, O and Q. The regulation lists the permitted letters, and those three are missing, because they are too easily confused with 1 and 0.

The four sections

PositionsWhat the regulation says they represent
1–3First section: identifies the manufacturer and the type of vehicle
4–8Second section: identifies attributes of the vehicle, such as model, body type and engine, as set out in the regulation's tables
9The check digit — a number or the letter X, used to verify the VIN was transcribed correctly
10Model year
11Plant of manufacture
12–17The number sequentially assigned by the manufacturer in production

For low-volume manufacturers the regulation handles the manufacturer identity and sequence number slightly differently, using positions 12–14 as part of the identifier and 15–17 for the sequence.

Let the decoder do the lookup

You do not need to memorize manufacturer codes. The NHTSA VIN Decoder lets you enter a VIN and returns the information encoded in it — including the build plant and country — as reported by the manufacturer. It also accepts partial VINs.

Using it as a fraud check

Decode the VIN you were given and compare it to the car in front of you and to the listing:

  • Does the model year match what the seller claims?
  • Does the engine and body type match the car?
  • Does the manufacturer match the badge on the car?

A VIN that decodes to a different model, a different year, or a different engine is either mistyped or belongs to another vehicle. Either way, you need the correct one before going further. See how VIN cloning is detected.

Then check the check digitPosition 9 lets you test mathematically whether the VIN is internally consistent. See how the check digit works.

What decoding tells you and what it doesn't

Decoding tells you what the car was built as. It says nothing about what has happened to it since — accidents, title brands, liens, mileage. For that you need the records filed against the VIN.

Decoding tells you what the car was built as. The history record tells you what happened to it.
