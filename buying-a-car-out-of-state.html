Buying a used car in another state

Last checked September 20, 2026

The best-priced example of the car you want is often somewhere else. Buying across state lines is routine, but it adds steps — and the rules for each step are set by the states involved, not nationally. The sequence below is the part that holds everywhere. The details you have to check with your own state's motor vehicle agency.

Why the history check matters more here

A car moving between states is exactly the situation in which title brands get lost. Brands are applied by state agencies under state rules, and a vehicle retitled in a state with different rules can come out with a cleaner-looking title than it deserves. See title brands and title washing.

So on an out-of-state purchase, a check of the current title alone is not enough. You want the title history across every state the car has been registered in — which is what federal title data through NMVTIS (US Dept. of Justice) and the commercial reports built on it are for.

The steps that apply in every state

  1. Confirm the seller is on the title. The name on the title should match the person or business selling the car. If it does not, stop until it is resolved — see title jumping.
  2. Check for liens. A lien recorded in the seller's state follows the car into yours. See liens.
  3. Find out what your state needs to register the car. This commonly includes the signed title, a bill of sale, proof of insurance, and sometimes a VIN inspection by a state official. Your state's motor vehicle agency publishes the list.
  4. Find out whether your state requires an inspection — safety, emissions, or both — before registration, and whether a car from out of state has to meet it.
  5. Understand where sales tax is paid. This depends on the states involved and on whether you buy from a dealer or a private party. Ask your state's agency before assuming.
  6. Plan how the car gets home legally. Driving an unregistered car across states may require a temporary permit or transit tag. Some buyers ship the car instead.
Ask before you buy, not afterA call to your own state's motor vehicle agency before you commit costs nothing, and it is the only reliable way to learn what they will demand when you arrive with an out-of-state title. Rules differ enough that advice from someone in another state may simply be wrong for yours.

If you are not going to see it in person

Arrange an independent inspection in the seller's city, chosen and paid for by you. Verify the car exists before any money moves. See verifying an online listing.

Emissions and equipment differences

Some states set their own vehicle emissions standards. A car originally sold into one market may or may not satisfy another state's registration requirements. If you live in a state with its own standards, check whether the specific vehicle can be registered there before you buy it.
