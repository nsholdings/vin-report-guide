Open recalls on used cars: who fixes them and what they cost

Last checked September 20, 2026

A recall notice goes to the registered owner at the time. If that owner ignored it, moved, or sold the car, the repair may never have been done — and the next owner inherits a known safety defect without knowing it.

The repair is free, including for a used-car buyer

NHTSA's published guidance is direct on this: once a defect is identified, "the manufacturer is then required to remedy the problem at no charge to the owner," and dealer agreements require dealers to honor the recall and remedy defects at no extra charge "regardless of where the vehicle or equipment was originally purchased." Source: NHTSA: Motor Vehicle Safety Defects and Recalls.

So an open recall on a car you bought privately, second-hand, from another state, is still fixed free at a franchised dealer for that brand.

How to find open recalls

Enter the VIN at NHTSA Recalls. It is free and takes seconds.

Know what it will not show, per NHTSA:

  • Recalls already repaired
  • Safety recalls more than 15 years old, except where a manufacturer offers longer coverage
  • Recalls by some small or specialty manufacturers
  • International vehicles

The age limit matters for older cars

NHTSA's recall guidance sets a limit on the free remedy: the vehicle cannot be more than 15 years old on the date the defect or noncompliance is determined, counted from the date of sale to the first purchaser. For tires, coverage applies only if the tires were bought within 5 years of the determination. Source: NHTSA: Motor Vehicle Safety Defects and Recalls.

So on an older car, a recall issued long ago may still be free to fix, but a defect identified after the car is past that age may not be.

Is an open recall a reason to walk away?

Usually not. An open recall is a known problem with a known, free fix. It is often a better situation than an unknown problem. What it should do is change the order of events:

  • Ask the seller whether they will have it repaired before the sale
  • If not, factor in the time to book the dealer appointment
  • For some recalls, parts are not immediately available and NHTSA may advise not driving the vehicle — read the specific recall notice
Read the specific recallSome recall notices instruct owners not to drive the vehicle or to park it outside until repaired. The lookup result links to the details. Read them before you drive it home.
