Buying from an online listing: verifying the car exists

Last checked September 13, 2026

The fraud that costs the most in a remote car purchase is not a bad car. It is no car — photos taken from a real listing, a story about being deployed overseas, and a request for a deposit through a service that cannot reverse it.

Verify the car before you verify the deal

1. Reverse image search every photo

If the same images appear in older listings in other cities, the photos were lifted. This takes two minutes and ends a large share of fake listings on its own.

2. Ask for a photo you specify

Not "send more photos" — a specific one that cannot be taken from an existing listing. The dashboard VIN plate through the windshield with today's newspaper or a written note in the frame. The odometer with the driver's door open. A corner of the engine bay with the seller's hand in the shot.

Someone who has the car can do this in a minute. Someone who does not will offer a reason.

3. Run the VIN before you spend anything

NICB VINCheck for theft, salvage and flood — free, five lookups per 24 hours per IP. NHTSA Recalls for open recalls — free. Then the full record: title history across states, the odometer sequence, liens.

Check that the report's basic facts match the listing. Wrong trim, wrong engine, wrong state, wrong year — any mismatch means the VIN and the photos are not the same car.

The full record costs a fraction of a plane ticket, and it is the check that disqualifies the most cars.

4. Check who the seller is

A dealer should have an address you can look at on a map, a phone number that connects to a business, and a licence number you can verify with the state. A private seller should have a name that matches the name on the title. If the seller is "handling the sale for a relative", the title is not in their name, and that needs resolving before money moves, not after.

The payment rules that do not bend

Never send money for a car you have not seen, to a person you have not verified.Wire transfers, gift cards, cryptocurrency, and payment apps between strangers are all effectively irreversible. So is a deposit to "hold" a car. Escrow services recommended by the seller are frequently controlled by the seller.

Specific stories that should end the conversation

  • The seller is overseas, deployed, or relocating urgently, and the car is in storage
  • The transaction will go through a third party the seller chose, including one branded as a well-known marketplace
  • The price is far below market and there is a reason attached that requires speed
  • The seller will not do a live video call showing the car and the VIN
  • The title is not in the seller's name and will be "sorted out at delivery"

If the car is real and you still cannot see it

Pay for a third-party pre-purchase inspection in the car's city, arranged by you, paid by you, with the report sent to you. It costs a fraction of what you are about to spend and it puts an independent person physically next to the car. If the seller resists an inspector they did not choose, you have your answer.
