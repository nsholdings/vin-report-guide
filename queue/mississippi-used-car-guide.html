Buying a used car in Mississippi: the flood brand that overrides everything

Last checked November 1, 2026

Mississippi's rule is simple at the front end: if the word salvage appears on the title, the vehicle has to be inspected. What makes it worth studying is the file that inspection demands, and one brand that outranks all the others.

Photographs taken before the repairs

For a salvage or rebuilt inspection, the applicant brings the original salvage title or insurance retention letter, the state's inspection form, original parts invoices, a work order, and four colour photographs of all four sides taken before repairs (Mississippi Department of Public Safety). Where the work order was performed by an individual rather than a shop, it must be notarised.

Pre-repair photographs are the single most useful thing a later buyer can ask for. They show the damage as it was, before anyone decided what to tell you about it.

The parts invoice rule

Each parts receipt must list the donor vehicle's VIN, year, make and model, the seller's name, address and signature, and the buyer's driving licence number for a private party or tax ID for a company. That is a tighter specification than most states use, and it means a Mississippi rebuild leaves a traceable parts trail.

Run the donor VINsEach donor VIN on those invoices is a car with its own history. A rebuild assembled from several heavily damaged donors is a different proposition from one that used new parts.

Donor VINs tell you what went into the car. The car's own VIN tells you what happened to it.

Flood outranks everything

Mississippi applies a flood brand to flood-damaged vehicles, and its own guidance is unusually firm about it: the flood brand can never be removed, and it overrides all other brands. Hail-damaged vehicles get their own hail brand.

For flood vehicles, the insurance company applies for the branded title in its own name, and the buyer then applies for a new title with the four pre-repair photographs, the form and the inspection — with a notarised work order for hail repairs performed by an individual.

If you see a Mississippi flood brand, treat the car as a flood car first and everything else second. Read flood-damaged cars; the failure modes are electrical, delayed and expensive. For hail, see hail-damaged cars, where the risk profile is very different.

The fees, and what they tell you

Salvage, rebuilt, flood and hail inspections cost $125. VIN verifications and bonded titles cost $50. Fees are payable by certified check or money order only. None of these amounts is large enough to justify skipping the process, so a Mississippi car that plainly was rebuilt but has no inspection record is a car with a gap in its paperwork.

Buying checklist for Mississippi

  1. Ask for the four pre-repair photographs. They exist, because the state required them.
  2. Read the parts invoices and run the donor VINs.
  3. Check specifically for a flood brand, which overrides everything else.
  4. For a bonded title, ask what happened to the original ownership documents.
  5. Pull a multi-state history — see title brands and title washing.

Check for flood and salvage records in every state before you view a Mississippi car.
