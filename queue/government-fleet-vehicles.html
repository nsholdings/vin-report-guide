Buying an ex-federal fleet vehicle and the SF-97

Last checked November 22, 2026

Former federal vehicles come up for sale in large numbers, and they have a reputation for being well kept and lightly optioned. The paperwork is unusual — there is no ordinary certificate of title — and the sale terms are more restrictive than most buyers expect. Here is what the government's own documents say.

Where the cars come from

GSA Fleet leases non-tactical vehicles to federal agencies, and "When we determine that a vehicle meets replacement criteria we offer it to the public for sale." GSA states that "Every year, 30,000+ vehicles are sold at" auction (GSA). Sales are open to the public.

The SF-97 instead of a title

The form is SF97-1, "The United States Government Certificate to Obtain Title to a Vehicle," revision date 04/2012 (GSA Forms Library). The federal regulation says to use it where "The motor vehicle will be retitled by a State, Commonwealth, territory or possession of the United States or the District of Columbia" and "The purchaser intends to operate the motor vehicle on highways" (41 CFR 102-34.305).

Two details matter at the counter. The same regulation notes that "The original SF 97 is printed on secure paper to identify readily any attempt to alter the form," that "The form is also pre-numbered to prevent duplicates," and that "State motor vehicle agencies may reject certificates showing erasures or strikeovers." And GSA's current sale terms state plainly that "The SF-97-1 is not a motor vehicle registration" (GSA Fleet sale terms, PDF). You take it to your state to be retitled; it is not itself the title.

Those terms also state that junk, salvage and flood-damage vehicles receive a branded SF-97-1. A government sale is not a guarantee of a clean history — see what the title brands mean.

A government sale is still a used car. Check its recorded history before you bid.

What the sale terms actually promise

Very little, and they say so. The warranty is that the property "will conform to its description only," and "Condition of property is not guaranteed." A refund for a mis-description is limited to the purchase price. GSA Fleet's internet terms put it more fully: "This warranty is in place of all other guarantees and warranties, express or implied," and "The Government does not warrant the merchantability of the property or its fitness for any use or purpose" (GSA Fleet, PDF).

The same document notes that "Vehicles with known open recalls have been identified, but additional outstanding recalls may be possible," and that "All telematics service subscriptions have been cancelled." Check recalls yourself — open recalls on used cars explains the free VIN search.

Deadlines are short and not negotiablePayment is due two business days from the award e-mail, "Property must be removed within ten (10) calendar days from the date and time of award e-mail notification," and "GSA does not grant extensions of time for payment and removal." The SF-97 "can only be made out to the individual purchasing the vehicle," so you cannot buy on someone else's behalf and have the paperwork follow.

The maintenance records question

This is where the common assumption breaks down. GSA does keep detailed repair history: on a leased fleet vehicle, "Repairs must be approved by MCC before any work begins," the Maintenance Control Center "tracks all vendor activity" in GSAFleet.gov and keeps a repair history for each vehicle, with a stated preventive-maintenance interval, for a vehicle without an oil life sensor, of "Without OLS: Change every 7,500 miles or once per year" (GSA).

But none of the GSA pages we could read says that per-vehicle maintenance history is handed to the buyer. The record exists inside the system; nothing we found connects it to the person who wins the auction. GSA's sales material describes the vehicles as "well maintained" and "carefully detailed before sale," with "low miles and only one previous owner" and leases "typically 3-5 years" — but a marketing description is not a service record. Do not pay a premium for documentation you have not actually been shown.

Vehicles sold for other agencies

Not every car in a GSA sale came out of the GSA Fleet lease programme. On consignments for other federal agencies, GSA states: "We cannot attest to the condition of vehicles we sell for other Federal agencies, however known deficiencies will be listed." Worth establishing which category a vehicle falls into before you bid.

Before you bid

  • Ask about inspection dates. The terms defer inspection to the custodian — "Please contact the custodian for inspection dates and times."
  • Run the VIN. The branded-SF-97 rule means salvage and flood vehicles do appear in these sales.
  • Check recalls separately, since GSA flags known ones but says more are possible.
  • Plan collection before you bid, given the ten-day removal rule.
  • Treat it like any other auction purchase. Buying a car at auction and former police, taxi and rideshare vehicles cover the adjacent cases.

Confirm the title and brand history independently of the auction paperwork.
