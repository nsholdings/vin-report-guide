Buying a used car in Nevada: 65%, and the orange title

Last checked November 1, 2026

Nevada's threshold is lower than most, its salvage title is a different colour from an ordinary one, and its rules put a hard stop on selling salvage cars to private buyers. All three matter if you are shopping there.

The 65% line

A vehicle is a total loss in Nevada when the cost of repair would exceed 65% of its pre-damage fair market value, not counting painting. Below that, it is not salvage. Vehicles ten or more model years old have a limited-repair exception (Nevada DMV).

Two details worth holding on to. The exclusion of paint from the calculation pushes more cars over the line than a naive reading suggests — paint is a large share of a modern body repair. And a definition that also captures any vehicle previously declared a total loss, flood-damaged or non-repairable anywhere means Nevada looks beyond its own borders.

The orange title, and what you may not do with it

Once declared a total loss, the vehicle needs an orange-coloured salvage title, applied for on Form VP 213 with a $10 fee and usually issued within a couple of business days.

Then the rule that catches people out: selling a salvage vehicle to a private individual is illegal until it has been rebuilt and inspected. A private seller offering you a Nevada car on a salvage title is proposing a transaction the state does not permit.

Colour is a five-second checkNevada prints salvage titles orange. If you are handed an orange document and told it is an ordinary title, you have learned something before reading a word of it.

Nevada looks at out-of-state total-loss records. A full history report shows you the same thing.

Repairs have to be authorised first on newer cars

For vehicles five model years old or newer, the owner needs DMV authorisation on Form VP 209 before repairs begin. The inspection itself is then two-stage: a Nevada-registered garage, licensed body shop or licensed rebuilder performs the professional safety inspection and completes Part I of the inspection form, and a DMV inspection station then carries out a final check verifying the VIN and the repair work.

Two independent parties therefore sign off on a newer Nevada rebuild. Ask to see both parts.

The brand, and the penalties behind it

The title is branded Rebuilt or another appropriate term, and the brand cannot be removed. Before selling or transferring a vehicle, the seller must disclose in writing whether it is salvage, rebuilt or reconstructed; failing to disclose, or lying about it, violates Nevada law (NRS 487.830). Tampering with or concealing a salvage or rebuilt brand is also illegal, and violations involving vehicles worth $250 or more are felonies.

Buying checklist for Nevada

  1. Look at the colour of the title before you read it.
  2. If it is salvage and the seller is a private individual, stop — that sale is not permitted.
  3. For a rebuilt car five model years old or newer, ask for the VP 209 authorisation and both inspection stages.
  4. Get the written salvage/rebuilt/reconstructed disclosure and keep it.
  5. Pull a multi-state history — see title brands and title washing and what a salvage title costs you.

Check for total-loss and flood records in every state before you travel to see a Nevada car.
