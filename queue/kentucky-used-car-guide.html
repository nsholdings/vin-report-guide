Buying a used car in Kentucky: the affidavit does the work

Last checked November 1, 2026

Kentucky approaches rebuilt vehicles from the paperwork end. There is no routine physical inspection for every car; instead the applicant has to prove, on paper, where every part came from. For a buyer, that paper is gold.

What a Kentucky rebuilder has to file

The application requires a completed TC Form 96-182 and TC Form 96-215, the Affidavit of Motor Vehicle Assembled from Wrecked or Salvaged Motor Vehicles, plus (601 KAR 9:200):

  • An address where the vehicle can be examined.
  • The assigned certificate of title — or a notarised affidavit of ownership history showing at least five years of ownership by the current owner and stating that no liens exist.
  • A notarised labour statement describing the repairs and the replaced parts.
  • Proof of purchase for each part, giving the seller's name, address and phone number, the purchase date, the price, and the serial number or donor VIN. Where a part has no serial number, a written explanation is required.
  • The licence plate, or a statement explaining why there is none.
  • A federal odometer disclosure, where not already on the application or title.
  • For motorcycles, a pencil tracing or photograph of the motor and frame identification numbers for both the rebuilt machine and the donor.
Ask for the parts fileKentucky required a receipt per part with a donor VIN on it. That file names the cars this car was assembled from, and you can run those VINs. It is the most informative document attached to any Kentucky rebuild.

Donor VINs tell you what went into the car. The car's own VIN tells you what happened to it.

When the State Police get involved

Kentucky requires a confidential inspection by the Kentucky State Police in two situations: where the required documentation is not available, or where an NCIC check flags the vehicle as stolen and a VIN check flags a non-conforming VIN. A confidential inspection examines a distinguishing number that is not readily visible.

In other words, the police inspection is the fallback for cars whose paperwork or identity does not hold up. If a Kentucky car went through one, ask why.

The 75% documentation rule

There is a second check in the other direction. Where the repair documentation comes to less than 75% of the vehicle's value, the Cabinet requires either an insurance company's written damage statement or a salvage pool receipt. That stops a rebuilder claiming a trivial repair on a car that was plainly written off.

The brand

A Kentucky rebuilt title carries a brand printed on its face. The wording is rebuilt vehicle, and where the car also carries a water-damage brand, the title reads rebuilt vehicle water damaged. If you see the longer phrase, treat it as a flood car first and a rebuilt car second — see flood-damaged cars.

Buying checklist for Kentucky

  1. Ask for TC 96-215 and the parts receipts filed with it.
  2. Run the donor VINs listed on those receipts.
  3. Ask whether a State Police confidential inspection was required, and why.
  4. Read the brand wording in full — the water-damage variant changes everything.
  5. Have the structure checked independently — see signs of frame damage and the pre-purchase inspection.

Check the title and brand history across states before you start on the parts file.
