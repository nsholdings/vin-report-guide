Buying a used car in Maine: five parts is the dividing line

Last checked November 1, 2026

Maine does something no other state in this series does. It decides which legend goes on the title by counting the component parts that were replaced.

The count

Under Maine's rules (29-A M.R.S. § 667):

Component parts replacedLegend
Five or morerebuilt salvage
One to fourrebuilt

And one exclusion that matters: airbags are excluded from that count.

What the exclusion means in practiceA car can have its airbags replaced — which only happens after a deployment, and therefore after a significant impact — without that contributing to the five-part threshold. So a Maine "rebuilt" legend is compatible with a crash severe enough to fire the restraints. See airbag deployment history.

Reading the two legends

Because the distinction is a count rather than a cost, the Maine legend tells you something unusually specific: roughly how extensive the physical rebuild was. Four replaced parts is a different car from twelve. Ask which parts, and get the list.

That list exists, because somebody had to count to decide which legend applied.

The legend summarises the rebuild. A history report shows the loss that caused it.

Who declares, and when

A vehicle is declared a salvage vehicle by reason of its condition or circumstance, and Maine names three parties who may make that declaration: the insurer, the owner, and the towing company. That third one is unusual, and it widens the range of cars that can end up on a salvage certificate.

  • An insurer must surrender the certificate of title to the Secretary of State and apply for a certificate of salvage within 30 days of settling the claim.
  • An owner must do the same before transferring the vehicle, unless it goes to a licensed recycler.

Why a count beats a percentage

Most states measure severity in money, which drifts with the car's value and with what a particular estimator thought a job would cost. Maine measures it in parts, which does not drift. The same impact produces the same count whether the car was worth $4,000 or $40,000 — so a Maine legend compares more cleanly across cars than a cost-threshold brand from another state does.

The disclosure you are owed

Where a repairer or rebuilder transfers the vehicle, they must disclose in writing to the transferee that it was a salvage vehicle and list the repairs made. That second half is the valuable part — a written list of repairs from the person who performed them.

Note the limit: the obligation falls on the repairer or rebuilder. A private seller further down the chain who did not rebuild the car is not covered by the same duty, so ask them directly and in writing.

Buying checklist for Maine

  1. Read the legend precisely: rebuilt, or rebuilt salvage.
  2. Ask for the list of component parts replaced.
  3. Ask separately whether the airbags were replaced — they are outside the count.
  4. If buying from a rebuilder, ask for the written disclosure and the repair list.
  5. Pull a multi-state history — see title brands and title washing.

Check the title and claim history against the VIN before you ask about the parts count.
