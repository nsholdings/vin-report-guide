Buying a car that was stolen and recovered

Last checked September 20, 2026

Some stolen cars are recovered — found abandoned, intact or partly stripped — and returned to the owner or the insurer, then sold. There is nothing illegal about buying one. But "stolen" in a car's record is a word that needs to be followed all the way through to "recovered".

Unrecovered versus recovered

NICB VINCheck reports whether a vehicle has been reported stolen and not recovered, based on records from participating insurers. It is free, with a limit of five searches per 24 hours per IP address. Only insurance records appear — police records do not.

An unrecovered theft report ends the conversationIf a car being offered to you shows as stolen and not recovered, do not buy it. Walk away and consider telling local police.

If it was recovered

Recovered theft vehicles often end up with the insurer that paid the claim, which then sells them, sometimes with a title brand depending on state rules and the car's condition. See title brands.

What to confirm:

  • The history record shows the theft and the recovery
  • The title is properly in the seller's name — see title jumping
  • The VIN on the dashboard, door jamb and any other location all match — thieves sometimes alter identification. See VIN cloning.

A theft record should be followed through to recovery and a clean transfer. The record shows both.

What to inspect

  • Ignition and steering column — damage from forced entry or hot-wiring
  • Door locks and windows — forced entry
  • Missing or mismatched parts — stolen cars are often stripped of wheels, seats, electronics or catalytic converters, and replaced with used parts
  • Keys — how many keys come with the car, and whether the security system recognises them
  • Warning lights — disturbed electronics often leave faults

Price it accordingly

A recovered theft vehicle that was stripped and rebuilt with used parts is not the same car as one recovered untouched a day later. The record and an inspection tell you which you have. See pre-purchase inspection.
