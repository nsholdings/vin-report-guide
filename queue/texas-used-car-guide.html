Buying a used car in Texas: salvage, nonrepairable and rebuilt salvage titles

Last checked October 4, 2026

Texas uses two damage categories, and the difference decides whether a wrecked car can ever be driven again. Add the end of routine safety inspections for most passenger vehicles in 2025, and a Texas buyer has fewer state checkpoints standing between them and a badly repaired car than they might expect.

Salvage and nonrepairable: the Texas definitions

The Texas Department of Motor Vehicles' Salvage and Nonrepairable manual gives the definitions used in Texas law:

  • A salvage motor vehicle is damaged or missing a major component part to the extent that the cost of repair, including parts and labor, exceeds the vehicle's actual value immediately before the damage.
  • A nonrepairable motor vehicle is damaged, wrecked or burned to the extent that its only residual value is as a source of parts or scrap metal.

The manual then sets the rule that matters to a buyer: a vehicle issued a Nonrepairable Vehicle Title after September 1, 2003 may not be rebuilt, retitled or registered, and its body and frame cannot be used to rebuild another vehicle. A salvage vehicle, by contrast, can be rebuilt and retitled.

If you see "nonrepairable" on the paperworkFor a title issued after September 1, 2003, the TxDMV manual says the vehicle cannot be registered again. Paying for one as a road car means paying for something you cannot legally drive.

What "Rebuilt Salvage" means on a Texas title

The same manual describes a rebuilt salvage title as a regular certificate of title for a prior salvage motor vehicle that has been rebuilt. The title is branded "Rebuilt Salvage", and the motor vehicle record carries remarks showing the prior status. The brand stays with the car, so it should show up on any later Texas title and in national title data.

To get that title, the owner submits a Rebuilt Vehicle Statement (Form VTR-61). On it, the rebuilder certifies the work and states that the component parts were legally obtained. If an engine, frame or body from another vehicle was used, ownership evidence for that part is required.

That gives you a useful question for the seller: who rebuilt the car, and can they show the parts receipts and the ownership documents for any major components? A rebuilder who did the job properly will usually have them. A seller who has no idea where the parts came from is telling you something too. For the wider decision, see is a rebuilt title ever worth buying?

No state safety inspection for most cars since 2025

TxDMV's registration page states that, as of January 1, 2025, non-commercial vehicles are no longer required to obtain a safety inspection before registration. Emissions testing still applies in 17 counties: Brazoria, Collin, Dallas, Denton, El Paso, Ellis, Fort Bend, Galveston, Harris, Johnson, Kaufman, Montgomery, Parker, Rockwall, Tarrant, Travis and Williamson.

For a buyer, the effect is simple. Outside those emissions counties, the state no longer requires anyone to look at a passenger car before it is registered. An independent pre-purchase inspection is the only mechanical check you can count on, and it matters more for a rebuilt car than for any other.

Texas title brands follow the car into national title data. Check the VIN before you look at the car in person.

Title Check: TxDMV's own recommendation

TxDMV runs a Title Check page that points buyers to approved providers of reports from the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS). The agency says such a report shows the vehicle's title history, including whether it was ever in the possession of a junk or salvage yard or declared a total loss by an insurer. The same page recommends a separate full vehicle history report and a mechanic's inspection as well — NMVTIS covers title events, not maintenance or accidents that never led to a title brand. After you buy: 30 days to title

TxDMV's buying and selling page says vehicles must be titled in the buyer's name within 30 days of the date of sale. The seller can file a Vehicle Transfer Notification to protect themselves from tickets and tolls incurred after the sale. Get a signed bill of sale with the date and the VIN on it.

Texas checklist

  1. Ask whether the car has ever had a salvage or nonrepairable title.
  2. Walk away from a post-2003 nonrepairable vehicle if you intend to drive it.
  3. For a rebuilt salvage car, ask who did the work and for the parts paperwork.
  4. Run an NMVTIS-based title check, then a full history report.
  5. Book an independent inspection; the state no longer does one for most cars.
