Buying a used car in Missouri: the highway patrol examination

Last checked November 1, 2026

Missouri's rebuilt process is a documentation exercise before it is a mechanical one. The examination is run through the Highway Patrol, and the thing that fails applicants most often is a receipt that does not say enough.

Where the examination happens

Examinations are scheduled through the Motor Vehicle Inspection Office at the local troop headquarters (Missouri State Highway Patrol). That is a law-enforcement facility, not a garage — a useful reminder of what the examination is primarily for. It establishes identity and lawful ownership of parts.

The receipt rule that matters to you

Missouri requires that every used part has a receipt listing the VIN of the vehicle the part came from. Not the part number, not a description — the donor vehicle's VIN.

That is unusually specific, and it hands a later buyer something concrete. If you can get the file the rebuilder submitted, you can see which cars this car is partly made of, and you can run those VINs too. A donor list that includes several heavily damaged vehicles tells you how extensive the rebuild really was.

Ask for the receipts, then read the VINsThis is the most useful single document in a Missouri rebuild, and it only exists because the state insisted on it. A seller who says the receipts are "somewhere" is telling you they did not keep the file they were required to assemble.

The donor VINs are only half the story. Run the car's own VIN for its title and brand history too.

What each category requires

Examination typeWhat the applicant brings
SalvageCorrectly assigned title, a DOR-551 form, and receipts in the owner's name for replaced parts
Rebuilt salvageCorrectly assigned title, a DOR-551, and a DOR-4808 Incomplete Transaction Notice completed by a Department of Revenue contract office
Abandoned on real estateA DOR-551 and receipts in the owner's name for replaced parts
Tow lot auctionA DOR-551, a DOR-4579 Abandoned Property Bill of Sale with raised seal, and parts receipts
Special constructionProof of ownership of the body and/or frame — an MSO, a title in the applicant's name, or a copy of a title with a descriptive bill of sale — plus a DOR-551 and receipts

The last row deserves attention. A special construction title means the vehicle was assembled rather than manufactured as a unit. It is a legitimate category, and it is also where a car with a confused history can end up. Ask which category the car went through.

The ten-year repair standard

Missouri requires that vehicles less than ten years old be restored to their pre-salvage condition. On cars older than that, the standard is looser — which is another reason the physical inspection matters more as a car ages. See the pre-purchase inspection and signs of frame damage.

Buying checklist for Missouri

  1. Ask which examination category the car went through — salvage, rebuilt salvage, abandoned, tow lot or special construction.
  2. Get the parts receipts and read the donor VINs.
  3. Check the car's age against the ten-year restoration standard.
  4. Pull a full title history, including other states — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Decide on price with what a salvage title costs you in mind.

Check the car's own title and brand history before you start reading donor receipts.
