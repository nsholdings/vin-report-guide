State inspection and emissions records: what they reveal

Last checked September 20, 2026

Many states require periodic safety inspections, emissions tests, or both. Which states, which vehicles, and how often varies — some states require neither. Where they exist, these inspections leave records, and those records are useful to a buyer for a reason that has nothing to do with emissions: they capture the odometer reading on a known date.

Why inspection records matter for mileage

Odometer rollback is defeated by a sequence of readings recorded by different parties over time. Inspection visits are one of the regular sources of those readings, alongside title transfers and service visits. A car whose inspection records show a higher mileage than its current dashboard has a problem the seller needs to explain. See odometer rollback.

Inspection and title records put dated odometer readings in the history. The sequence is what exposes rollback.

What else they can show

  • Failed inspections — a car that failed emissions repeatedly may have an unresolved engine or exhaust problem
  • Gaps — a car in an inspection state with no inspections for years was possibly off the road, unregistered, or elsewhere
  • Location — inspections in states the seller never mentioned

Check what your state will require

If you are buying a car to register in a state with inspection requirements, find out before you buy whether it will need to pass one to be registered — and whether a car from another state faces additional requirements. A car that cannot pass your state's emissions test can be expensive to fix or impossible to register. Your state motor vehicle or environmental agency publishes the rules. See buying in another state.

Warning lights and readinessEmissions tests commonly depend on the car's onboard diagnostics. A check-engine light, or diagnostics that were recently cleared by disconnecting the battery or erasing codes, can cause a test to fail or be refused. A seller who cleared codes just before your visit is hiding the signal the test relies on.

Where to find the records

Inspection records may appear in a commercial history report, depending on the state and the report provider. Some states also offer their own lookup for inspection history. The seller may have paper inspection certificates as well — ask for them.
