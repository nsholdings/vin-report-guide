Buying a used car in Minnesota: the prior salvage brand

Last checked November 1, 2026

Most states brand a car for what happened inside their own borders. Minnesota goes further: it has a brand for what happened somewhere else.

The prior salvage brand

Minnesota applies a prior salvage brand to a vehicle that had a salvage certificate of title or brand issued at any time in its history by any other jurisdiction (Minn. Stat. § 168A.151). It applies to vehicles not caught by the main salvage requirement.

That is a direct answer to title washing, which is the practice of moving a branded car to a state that will not carry the brand forward. Minnesota looks at the car's whole history and records what it finds. See title brands and title washing for how the problem works generally.

Read this both waysA Minnesota prior salvage brand does not mean the car was wrecked in Minnesota. It means some jurisdiction branded it, at some point. Ask which one and when — that is where the real story is.

When the full salvage brand applies

  • Insurers that pay damages on a vehicle must promptly title it as salvage or stamp the existing title. For a late-model or high-value vehicle, the brand applied is salvage.
  • Other buyers must promptly obtain a salvage title for a damaged late-model or high-value vehicle where, among other conditions, the repair cost exceeds its value.
  • Self-insured owners are caught where damage exceeds 80 percent of actual cash value.
  • Unrepairable total losses go a different route entirely and require a junking certificate.

The self-insured 80 percent figure is worth holding on to, because it tells you roughly where Minnesota draws the line on severity even where the statute otherwise speaks in terms of value rather than percentages.

Minnesota records brands from other states. A multi-state title history is how you see the same thing yourself.

What a junking certificate means for a buyer

If a vehicle is an unrepairable total loss, it goes to a junking certificate rather than a salvage title. A car on that path is not headed back to the road. If a seller is describing a Minnesota car in those terms while also offering to sell it to you as a driver, the two statements do not fit together.

The practical consequence for shopping

Because Minnesota carries forward other states' brands, a clean Minnesota title is a stronger signal than a clean title in a state with no such rule. That is a genuine advantage — but only for the period the car has been in Minnesota. A car that arrived last month has not had its history tested by anything.

So ask how long the car has been titled in Minnesota, and compare that against the title history. A long Minnesota history with no brand is meaningful. A short one is not.

Buying checklist for Minnesota

  1. Check for the prior salvage brand as carefully as for a salvage brand.
  2. If it is there, ask which jurisdiction branded the car and in what year.
  3. Ask how long the car has been titled in Minnesota.
  4. Pull a multi-state title history rather than relying on the current title alone.
  5. Inspect it independently — see the pre-purchase inspection and why a clean title does not mean no accidents.

Check every state the car has been titled in, not just the one on the paperwork in front of you.
