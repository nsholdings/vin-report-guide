VIN cloning: how it works and how to catch it

Last checked September 20, 2026

VIN cloning is the fraud where a stolen car is given the identity of a legitimate one. The thief copies a real VIN — often from a car of the same make, model and color, sometimes photographed straight through someone's windshield — onto the stolen vehicle, and sells it with that car's clean history.

Why the quick checks don't catch it

  • The VIN is real, so it decodes correctly with the NHTSA VIN Decoder
  • The check digit is valid — see how the check digit works
  • The history report is clean, because it is the history of the legitimate car
  • NICB VINCheck shows no theft report — against the cloned VIN. The stolen car's own VIN is the one reported.

Every lookup is being run against the wrong car.

What does catch it

1. The VIN appears in more than one place on the car

The dashboard plate is the easiest to replace. Compare it against the door-jamb label and, where you can find it, the number stamped elsewhere on the body or frame. A cloned car often has one location changed and another overlooked. See where the VIN appears.

2. The plate and label themselves

  • Dashboard VIN plate rivets that are scratched, mismatched, or replaced with something other than the factory type
  • A door-jamb label that is peeling, misaligned, or looks newer than the paint around it
  • Characters in a font or spacing that differs from other VINs on that make

3. The record doesn't fit the car

This is where the history report earns its money even against a clone. Read it for details that should match the car in front of you:

  • Where the car has been registered, against where the seller says it has been
  • The last recorded odometer reading, against the dashboard
  • Equipment and trim as decoded, against what the car actually has
  • Recent registration activity in a distant state — the legitimate car may still be on the road there

A clone copies the number, not the history. Details in the record that don't fit the car are how cloning is caught.

4. The title

Cloned cars are often sold with forged or fraudulently obtained titles. Check that the title's VIN, name and security features look right, and that the seller's ID matches the name on it.

The price is the first clueCloned cars are stolen, so they are usually priced below market with an urgent reason attached. A late-model car well below its value, for cash, today, is the pattern.

If you suspect it

Do not confront the seller. Walk away, and report what you saw to local police or to the NICB.
