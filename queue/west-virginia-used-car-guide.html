Buying a used car in West Virginia: the reconstructed title

Last checked November 1, 2026

West Virginia calls a rebuilt car a reconstructed vehicle, and the process has a detail that tells you how seriously the state takes the restriction on driving a salvage car: you need a permit from the State Police simply to drive it to its inspection.

Getting the car there

The vehicle must be examined by a West Virginia DMV reconstructed vehicle inspector. It can be driven to that inspection only with a one-trip permit issued by the West Virginia State Police — otherwise it must be towed or hauled (West Virginia DMV).

For a buyer, that is a useful fact in a practical sense. If someone offers to let you test drive a West Virginia car that is still on a salvage title, they are proposing something the state does not allow.

The two forms

FormWho completes it
DMV-SV-3 — Reconstructed Vehicle ExaminationCompleted by the owner, signed by the DMV inspector
DMV-202-TR — Certificate of InspectionCompleted by the inspector

Alongside those, the owner submits the salvage certificate of title, properly assigned, with the odometer disclosure section completed. The inspection fee is $35, plus any other applicable fees, payable with the application.

The odometer section is a dated third-party readingWest Virginia requires the odometer disclosure on the assigned title. That reading, with its date, belongs in the sequence you check for rollback — see odometer rollback: how to check the numbers.

One recorded reading is a data point. A history report assembles the whole sequence.

What the title says afterwards

The vehicle must be retitled as a reconstructed vehicle before it can be registered for use on a public highway, and the owner then receives a reconstructed certificate of title. The word to look for on a West Virginia document is therefore reconstructed, not rebuilt or salvage-rebuilt.

That matters when the car later moves to another state. A different state may translate the brand into its own vocabulary, or may carry it forward with West Virginia named as the issuing jurisdiction. Either way the underlying event is the same — see title brands and title washing.

One more practical point about the forms. Because the DMV-SV-3 is completed by the owner but signed by the inspector, it records what the owner said about the rebuild as well as the inspector's sign-off. Reading it tells you how the rebuilder described their own work at the time, which is a more reliable account than a description produced for a sale.

What the inspection does not do

As in every state covered in this series, the examination establishes identity and that the work was done. It does not certify that the structure was returned to specification. If the car took a significant hit, that question is still open, and the only way to answer it is an independent inspection on a lift — see the pre-purchase inspection and signs of frame damage.

Buying checklist for West Virginia

  1. Confirm which document you are being shown: salvage certificate, or reconstructed title.
  2. Ask for the DMV-SV-3 and DMV-202-TR.
  3. Check the odometer disclosure on the assigned title against the dashboard.
  4. Decline to drive a car still on a salvage title.
  5. Pull a multi-state history before travelling to see it — see before you travel to buy a car.

Check the title and brand history against the VIN before you arrange to see the car.
