Boats don't have a VIN: checking a Hull Identification Number

Last checked September 20, 2026

If you are buying a boat, the number you are looking for is not a VIN. Recreational boats built for the US market carry a Hull Identification Number, or HIN, under federal rules. It does the same job — it ties the hull to its records — and it can be checked for the same kinds of tampering.

The format

Per 33 CFR Part 181, Subpart C (Hull Identification Numbers), a HIN "must consist of twelve characters, uninterrupted by slashes, hyphens, or spaces."

CharactersMeaning
1–3Manufacturer identification code
4–8Serial number assigned by the manufacturer (the letters I, O and Q are excluded)
9–10Month and year of certification, or of manufacture — the month is a letter, starting with A for January, and character 10 is the last digit of the year
11–12Model year

A HIN that is not 12 characters, or contains separators, is not in the current federal format. Very old boats may predate it — which is a reason for more care, not less.

There are two of them

The regulation requires "two identical hull identification numbers" on each hull:

  • The primary HIN — on boats with transoms, on the starboard outboard side of the transom, within two inches of the top of the transom, gunwale or hull/deck joint, whichever is lowest
  • The duplicate — "in an unexposed location on the interior of the boat or beneath a fitting or item of hardware"

The HIN must be permanently affixed so that "alteration, removal, or replacement would be obvious."

Find both and compare themThe duplicate exists precisely so that a thief who alters the visible number is caught by the hidden one. If you cannot find the second HIN, or it does not match the first, stop. Ask the seller where the duplicate is — a legitimate owner may know; the manufacturer can tell you where they place it.

What to check against

  • The HIN on the title or registration — character by character
  • The model year in characters 11–12 against the year the seller claims
  • Signs of tampering: fresh gelcoat or paint around the transom plate, a plate that has been riveted or glued rather than molded in, characters that look re-stamped

Boats trailer, and trailers have VINs

If the sale includes a trailer, that trailer is a separate vehicle with its own VIN and, in most states, its own title or registration. Check it the same way you would a car's. See VIN basics.

Selling the boat with a trailer? The trailer's VIN carries its own title, lien and theft record.

Titling differs by state

Whether boats are titled, registered, or both — and which agency handles it — depends on the state. Some states title boats only above a certain size or engine power. Ask your state's boating or motor vehicle agency what documents will prove ownership before you pay.
