Buying a used car in Kansas: what the 75% calculation leaves out

Last checked November 1, 2026

Kansas uses a 75% threshold like many states. What makes it worth reading closely is what Kansas takes out of the calculation before applying it.

The exclusions

For a late model vehicle, a salvage vehicle is one wrecked or damaged so that the cost of repair reaches 75% or more of the fair market value immediately before the damage. But (K.S.A. 8-197):

  • Exterior cosmetic damage from windstorm or hail does not count towards the threshold at all.
  • Repair costs exclude tires, sound systems, and sales tax on parts or materials.

Those are small-sounding carve-outs with real effects. Stripping tax and tyres out of a repair bill lowers the number, which keeps some cars on the clean side of the line. Excluding hail and windstorm cosmetic damage keeps a whole category of write-offs off the title entirely.

Hail history may be invisible on a Kansas titleKansas sits in a part of the country that gets severe hail. A car written off for cosmetic hail damage is not a salvage vehicle under this definition. Check the roof, bonnet and boot lid in raking light, and read hail-damaged cars.

A hail write-off can leave a clean title and an insurance record. A history report is where that record appears.

Two other Kansas categories

TermWhat it covers
Salvage vehicle (non-late-model)A vehicle other than a late model vehicle, of a type required to be registered, that cannot be registered because of wreck or damage and needs substantial repairs
Salvage vehicle (insurer)A vehicle the insurer determines is a total loss and for which the insurer takes title
Nonhighway vehicleA vehicle that cannot be registered because it was not manufactured for highway use and lacks required equipment — also covers ATVs, work-site utility vehicles, micro utility trucks and recreational off-highway vehicles
Rebuilt salvage vehicleAny motor vehicle or travel trailer previously issued a salvage title

The insurer limb matters. A Kansas car only lands there if the insurer took title. Where the owner kept the car after a payout, that route does not engage, and whether a brand appears depends on the other definitions.

There is a second consequence worth drawing out. Because the threshold is measured against fair market value immediately before the damage, the same repair bill produces different outcomes on different cars. A cheap car crosses 75% on a modest repair; an expensive one may absorb serious structural work and stay below it. The brand reflects the arithmetic, not the severity.

Reading a rebuilt salvage title

Kansas defines a rebuilt salvage vehicle simply as one previously issued a salvage title. The brand therefore tells you the car was once salvage. It does not, on its own, tell you how badly it was damaged or how well it was repaired — see whether a rebuilt title is worth it.

Buying checklist for Kansas

  1. Ask what the damage was, specifically, and whether an insurer took title.
  2. Assume hail and windstorm cosmetic damage will not appear on the title. Inspect for it.
  3. If the title says rebuilt salvage, ask for the repair invoices and donor-part documentation.
  4. Pull a multi-state history — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Have the structure checked — see signs of frame damage.

Check the claim, title and auction history before you decide what a Kansas car is worth.
