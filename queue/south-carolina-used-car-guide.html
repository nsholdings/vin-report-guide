Buying a used car in South Carolina: the brand names the cause

Last checked November 1, 2026

South Carolina does something most states do not: it puts the cause of the loss into the title brand itself. If you learn the vocabulary, the title tells you where to start looking.

The brand set

A declared total loss is marked Salvage. Where the car can be rebuilt, the title may instead read (SCDMV):

BrandWhat it tells you to inspect
Salvage RebuiltStructure, panel alignment, repair quality
Salvage Fire / Salvage Fire RebuiltWiring, seals, heat-affected structure — see fire-damaged cars
Salvage Flood / Salvage Flood RebuiltCorrosion and electrics that fail later — see flood-damaged cars

Fire and flood are separated out for a reason. Both do damage that a cosmetic repair hides and that surfaces months or years later. A brand that names them is doing you a favour.

The 75% line, and the exceptions that matter more

A loss of 75% or more of fair market value must be declared a total loss. But South Carolina also lets insurers declare a total loss below that line, and it treats low-value cars separately: where fair market value is under $2,000, the insurer can declare it by submitting a particular form.

The exceptions run the other way too. A car is not marked salvage if it is marked junk, if the damage was under 75% with no water or fire damage, if it is worth under $2,000, or if it is titled as an antique.

Cheap cars and old cars fall out of the schemeUnder $2,000 in value, or titled as an antique, and the salvage brand may never be applied. On those cars the title proves very little, and the inspection proves everything. See why a clean title does not mean no accidents.

The brand names the cause. A history report shows the claim, the auction and the odometer entries behind it.

How the paperwork moves

Where the insurer takes the vehicle, it applies for the salvage title in the company's name with the title fee paid. Where the owner keeps the car after a payout, the insurer or its agent submits the application in the owner's name along with the title, the fee, and a letter stating the vehicle was declared a total loss and remained with the owner.

An owner who keeps a salvage vehicle may rebuild it and submit documentation to change the brand to Salvage Rebuilt. Note the wording: the brand changes. It does not disappear.

Buying checklist for South Carolina

  1. Read the brand phrase in full — the words fire and flood are the ones that change your inspection.
  2. If the car is cheap or antique-titled, assume the title may be silent and inspect accordingly.
  3. For a flood brand, check the wiring looms, under-carpet areas and seat rails before anything else.
  4. Pull a multi-state title history — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Price it as a branded car — see what a salvage title costs you.

Check the title history for fire, flood and salvage records before you negotiate.
