Buying an imported or grey-market vehicle

Last checked September 20, 2026

Some cars on the US market were never built for it: imported classics, enthusiast models from other markets, vehicles brought in by previous owners. Buying one is legitimate. Buying one that was not imported properly can leave you with a vehicle you cannot register.

The federal safety rule

From NHTSA's importation FAQs: "In order to be lawfully manufactured or imported for sale in the U.S., a motor vehicle must comply with all applicable FMVSS" — the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards. A nonconforming vehicle under 25 years old "cannot be lawfully imported into the U.S. on a permanent basis unless NHTSA determines it eligible for importation." Source: NHTSA importation FAQs.

The 25-year rule

NHTSA also states: "A motor vehicle that is at least 25 years old can be lawfully imported into the U.S. without regard to whether it complies" with the safety standards, and "the 25 year period runs from the date of the vehicle's manufacture." This is why so many imported classics are just over 25 years old.

Registered Importers

Newer nonconforming vehicles have to go through a Registered Importer — businesses "specifically approved by NHTSA to import nonconforming vehicles and to perform the necessary modifications" — or through an individual who has contracted with one.

Emissions are a separate question

The EPA regulates vehicle emissions separately under the Clean Air Act, which the agency says prohibits importing a vehicle "that does not conform". EPA has its own provisions for older vehicles, with different conditions from NHTSA's. Source: EPA: importing vehicles and engines. A vehicle that satisfies the safety rule does not automatically satisfy the emissions one.

What to ask the seller for

  • The import documentation — how, when and through whom it entered the US
  • For a vehicle under 25 years old, evidence it was brought into compliance through a Registered Importer
  • Emissions compliance documentation
  • A US title in the seller's name
If the paperwork is missingA vehicle that was never properly imported may be impossible to register, and in some cases may be subject to seizure. The discount on a car with incomplete import papers is not a bargain.

VINs on imported vehicles

Vehicles built for other markets may carry identification numbers that differ from the 17-character US standard, especially older ones. US history databases are built around US titles, so a recently imported vehicle may have little or no US record. The history that matters is the US title history since import. See how to decode a VIN.

Once a vehicle has a US title, its record across states is what shows whether it has been branded or financed.

Check registrability first

Before you pay, ask your state motor vehicle agency what it needs to title and register this specific vehicle. Some states have their own emissions standards that add another layer. See buying in another state.
