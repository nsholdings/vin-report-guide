Buying a used car in Alabama: who is allowed to rebuild it

Last checked November 1, 2026

Alabama's rule that matters most to a buyer is not about damage. It is about who is allowed to turn a salvage car back into a titled one — because that determines who you are actually dealing with.

Only two kinds of applicant

A prior owner may apply for a rebuilt inspection. Anyone else needs a master dealer regulatory licence (Alabama Department of Revenue). Applications go in electronically through the state's online system, with electronic payment only, and the inspection fee is $90 — $75 for the inspection plus $15 for title issuance.

So an Alabama rebuilt car came through either the person who owned it when it was wrecked, or a licensed dealer. If a seller's account of the rebuild does not fit one of those two descriptions, something in the story is wrong.

The threshold and the dead ends

A vehicle becomes salvage when the damage is greater than or equal to 75 percent of the fair retail value before the damage. From there the paths diverge sharply:

  • Salvage — may be rebuilt, inspected and retitled.
  • Parts only, parts car, non-rebuildable, certificate of destruction, junk — these brands mean the vehicle cannot be rebuilt. It exists to be sold for parts.

Alabama also states that a salvage title cannot be issued once a current salvage title already exists — a safeguard against a second, cleaner-looking document appearing alongside the first.

Read which brand, not just "branded"The gap between salvage and non-rebuildable in Alabama is the gap between a car you can register and one you never can. Sellers sometimes use the words loosely. The title does not.

Check whether a non-rebuildable or junk brand exists anywhere in the car's history before you pay.

What the rebuild has to achieve

Alabama requires that every salvage vehicle be restored in Alabama to the operating condition it was in before the event that caused the salvage title, and repairs must be finished before applying for the inspection. Until a rebuilt title is issued, a salvage vehicle cannot be registered or operated on the state's highways or other public places.

Inspections are scheduled by the Motor Vehicle Inspector once the application is processed, and the inspector then contacts the applicant to arrange where it happens.

What to ask an Alabama seller

  1. Which brand is on the title, word for word.
  2. Who applied for the rebuilt inspection — the prior owner, or a licensed dealer?
  3. Can you see the rebuilt inspection paperwork and the repair receipts?
  4. Was the car ever titled in another state, and under what brand? See title brands and title washing.
  5. Will an independent mechanic confirm the structural repair? See signs of frame damage.

And if you are buying from a private seller who turns out to be rebuilding and reselling cars regularly, read curbstoning — Alabama's licensing requirement is exactly the rule such a seller is working around.

Check the brand history across states before you accept an Alabama rebuilt title at face value.
