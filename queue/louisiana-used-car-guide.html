Buying a used car in Louisiana: the certificate of destruction

Last checked November 1, 2026

Louisiana has a title category that most states do not: a certificate of destruction aimed squarely at water-damaged vehicles. It exists because of what storms do to cars there, and it is the first thing to check on any Louisiana car.

What a certificate of destruction covers

A certificate of destruction is the title type for a water-damaged vehicle — other than an antique vehicle or one over 20,000 lb GVWR — or for a vehicle whose power train, computer or electrical system was damaged by governor-declared flooding and which was declared a total loss (Louisiana DPS, citing R.S. 32:702).

Notice how specific that second limb is. It names the three systems that flooding ruins and that a cosmetic clean-up can hide: power train, computer, electrical.

It is a one-way doorA vehicle holding a certificate of destruction cannot be issued a salvage or reconstructed title (R.S. 32:707.3). There is no paperwork route back to the road. A seller offering such a car as a repairable project is offering something that cannot be registered.

The 75% line

Louisiana defines a total loss as a vehicle with damages equivalent to 75% or more of the market value, determined by the most current J.D. Power Guide. Naming a specific valuation guide is unusual, and it makes the test less arguable than "fair market value" alone.

Flood records are the ones that travel worst between states. Check the full title history, not just the current title.

The disclosure form

Transfers of vehicles with salvage, reconstructed or certificate-of-destruction titles are governed by R.S. 32:706.1, and an insurance company applying for a certificate of destruction must submit the state's Disclosure of Salvage / Reconstructed / Water Damaged / Hail Damaged Vehicle form.

The title of that form is itself informative: Louisiana treats water damage and hail damage as distinct, named categories worth disclosing. If you are buying in Louisiana, ask which of those four words applies.

Why this matters outside Louisiana too

Flood-damaged cars travel. After a major storm, water-damaged vehicles move across state lines and are retitled where the brand does not follow. A buyer in a landlocked state can end up with a Gulf Coast flood car and a clean-looking title.

That is why a multi-state history matters more than the document in front of you — see title brands and title washing. And if there is any flood history at all, read flood-damaged cars before you go any further: the failures are electrical, they are delayed, and they are expensive.

Buying checklist for Louisiana

  1. Ask directly whether a certificate of destruction has ever been issued for the vehicle.
  2. Ask which of salvage, reconstructed, water damaged or hail damaged was disclosed.
  3. Check whether the loss date lines up with a named storm.
  4. Inspect for water lines, silt in the spare wheel well, and corrosion on seat rails and connectors.
  5. Get an independent inspection — see the pre-purchase inspection.

Check for flood, salvage and destruction records against the VIN before you view the car.
