Buying a used car in New Hampshire: inspected against the adjuster's report

Last checked November 1, 2026

New Hampshire's salvage inspection has an unusually concrete standard. It does not just confirm the car exists and the parts are not stolen. It verifies that the damage listed in the adjuster's report has been repaired.

Why that wording helps you

If the inspection is measured against the adjuster's report, then the adjuster's report is part of the file (New Hampshire DMV). That document is the insurer's own itemised account of what was wrong with the car — written before anyone had a reason to describe the damage charitably.

Ask for it. It is the most informative piece of paper in a New Hampshire rebuild, and it lets you check the repair line by line rather than taking a seller's summary.

Compare the report against the carTake the adjuster's list and walk the car with it. Each item should correspond to something you can see repaired. Items on the list with no visible work done to them are the conversation to have.

No percentage — just the insurer's call

New Hampshire defines a salvage vehicle as one deemed a total loss by an insurance company as a result of damage or theft. There is no published percentage threshold; the trigger is the insurer's declaration.

Theft sits in that definition alongside damage, which means a New Hampshire salvage title can exist on a car that was stolen, written off while missing, and later recovered — see buying a car that was stolen and recovered.

The adjuster's report covers one event. A history report covers everything else the car has been through.

The inspection, step by step

  • A vehicle on a salvage title cannot be registered or titled until it has been rebuilt for road use.
  • The inspection verifies the VIN and that the adjuster's listed damage has been repaired.
  • Owners schedule through the Title Bureau and use an inspection location from the state's list.
  • The inspection scheduling fee is $50, payable by Visa or MasterCard only. The salvage certificate application fee is a separate $10, payable by check or money order.

Two different inspections

This is the point people trip over. A salvage inspection does not replace a safety inspection, which is due within ten days of registration. Passing the salvage inspection gets the car titled. It does not mean the car has passed New Hampshire's ordinary roadworthiness check.

The brands

The salvage title itself is branded Salvage. After inspection and registration, later titles carry the legend Rebuilt Vehicle. Two different words at two different stages — worth knowing when you are reading a photograph of a document a seller has sent you.

Buying checklist for New Hampshire

  1. Ask for the insurance adjuster's report and read it against the car.
  2. Check which brand the document carries: Salvage, or Rebuilt Vehicle.
  3. Ask whether the loss was damage or theft.
  4. Confirm the separate safety inspection has been done or can be.
  5. Pull a multi-state history — see title brands and title washing.

Check for salvage, theft and total-loss records in every state before you view the car.
