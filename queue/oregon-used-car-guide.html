Buying a used car in Oregon: brands that stack

Last checked November 1, 2026

Most states show you a title brand. Oregon shows you a sequence. Its rules say what happens when a branded car is damaged again, and the answer is usually that the brands accumulate rather than overwrite.

How the stacking works

Oregon's rule sets out the behaviour precisely (OAR 735-024-0025):

  • A vehicle with a brand other than totaled that is later reported totaled generally has the totaled brand replace the old one. A flood-branded car later totalled and then titled as reconstructed becomes totaled-reconstructed.
  • But for assembled, glider kit, Lemon Law Buyback, reconstructed or replica vehicles, the original brand is kept and the totaled brand is added. A replica later reported totalled and then titled as reconstructed becomes replica-totaled-reconstructed.

A hyphenated Oregon brand is therefore a compressed history. Read every segment — each one is a separate event.

Once placed, a brand staysOregon's rule is that a brand will appear on any subsequent Oregon title issued for the vehicle, subject to narrow exceptions. The title also names the jurisdiction that issued the brand, unless Oregon issued it.

Where Oregon gets its information

Oregon will brand a title when the existing title or the national record already carries a brand, when a manufacturer gives notice to inscribe Lemon Law Buyback, when the vehicle meets the definition of an assembled, reconstructed, replica or totaled vehicle, when the DMV determines from a prior record — including one from another jurisdiction — that a brand should be placed, or when the national system holds a salvage-type report meeting the DMV's criteria that has not been amended or retracted.

That fourth and fifth route are the useful ones for a buyer. Oregon is actively reading other states' records rather than taking the paperwork in front of it at face value. See title brands and title washing for why that matters.

Oregon reads the national record. A history report lets you read the same thing before you buy.

When a brand comes off

Removal is possible but narrow: where an incorrect brand was issued, including where the originating jurisdiction says its own title was wrong; where the brand was placed in error; and where a vehicle reported totaled because of theft is recovered and no longer meets the statutory definition of a totaled vehicle.

That last one is worth understanding. A stolen car can be written off while it is missing and then turn up intact. If an Oregon car's history shows a totaled brand that was later removed, theft recovery is the likely explanation — see buying a car that was stolen and recovered.

Buying checklist for Oregon

  1. Read the whole brand string, segment by segment, and ask what each one refers to.
  2. Note which jurisdiction is named as the issuer — it may not be Oregon.
  3. If a brand was removed, ask why, and look for a theft record.
  4. Where the brand includes "reconstructed", get the repair documentation.
  5. Inspect independently — see the pre-purchase inspection.

Check every jurisdiction in the car's title history, not just the state on the current document.
