Buying a used car in Illinois: salvage certificates, REBUILT titles and the rebuilt disclosure

Last checked October 4, 2026

Illinois restricts who can own a salvage vehicle, marks rebuilt cars on the title, and requires a written disclosure when a rebuilt car is sold. Those rules give a private buyer some useful warning signs, if you know what to look for.

Who can take a salvage certificate in Illinois

Under the Illinois Vehicle Code, section 3-118(c), a salvage certificate may be assigned to a person licensed under the Code as a rebuilder, automotive parts recycler or scrap processor, or to an out-of-state salvage vehicle buyer. A junking certificate, by contrast, may be assigned to anyone.

The practical point: an ordinary private buyer is not on that list. If someone offers to sell you a car on an Illinois salvage certificate, the transfer the law describes is to a licensed business, not to you. Treat that offer as a red flag, along with any seller who is not named on the paperwork — see title jumping and curbstoning.

The same section requires that each application for a salvage certificate be verified against the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS) before the Secretary of State issues it. That is one reason Illinois salvage events should be visible in national title data.

The REBUILT notation

Section 3-118.1 says that whenever a certificate of title is issued for a vehicle that previously had a salvage certificate, the new title shall bear the notation "REBUILT".

There is one narrow exception: an insurer or a qualifying licensed dealer may exchange a salvage certificate for a title without the REBUILT notation when the salvage certificate was issued because of a total-loss theft claim and the vehicle was recovered without structural damage from collision, fire, flood, theft, rust or corrosion. So a recovered theft car can, in that situation, come back with a clean Illinois title. See recovered theft vehicles.

The Secretary of State's inspection

Section 3-308 says the Secretary of State shall inspect any vehicle 8 model years of age or newer for which an application for a rebuilt title will be submitted. The inspection is aimed at whether identification numbers have been removed, altered or tampered with, whether the application information is true, and whether the vehicle or its parts were stolen.

It is an anti-theft and paperwork check. It does not grade the quality of the repairs, and vehicles older than 8 model years fall outside the inspection requirement in that section. Get your own pre-purchase inspection.

A REBUILT notation should appear in national title data. Check the VIN before you meet the seller.

The Disclosure of Rebuilt Vehicle Status

Section 5-104.3 makes it an offense to knowingly, with intent to defraud or deceive, sell a vehicle with a rebuilt title unless it is accompanied by a Disclosure of Rebuilt Vehicle Status form, properly signed and delivered to the buyer. The same section bars dealers and rebuilders from selling a rebuilt vehicle titled in another jurisdiction without first obtaining an Illinois title marked "REBUILT".

If you are buying a car you know or suspect is rebuilt, ask for the disclosure form. A seller who says "it's just a regular title" while the VIN history shows a salvage event deserves a much closer look. For the buying decision itself, see is a rebuilt title ever worth buying?

Emissions testing

The Illinois Environmental Protection Agency says emissions testing applies in the Chicago and Metro-East St. Louis areas, and that most 1996 and newer gasoline-powered passenger vehicles are subject to it after they are four years old. It also notes that the Secretary of State's office denies plate registration to non-complying vehicles. If you live in a testing area, ask the seller when the car was last tested.

Illinois checklist

  1. Don't accept a salvage certificate as a private buyer.
  2. Read the title for "REBUILT".
  3. Ask for the Disclosure of Rebuilt Vehicle Status if the car has been rebuilt.
  4. Remember the state inspection checks identity and theft, not repair quality.
  5. Run a VIN check and get an independent inspection.
