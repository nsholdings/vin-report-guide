Buying a used car in Colorado: the hail exclusion

Last checked November 1, 2026

Colorado's salvage law contains three words that change how you should read a Colorado title: excluding hail damage. Hail is carved out of the definition, in a state that gets a great deal of it.

What Colorado counts as salvage

A salvage vehicle is one damaged by collision, fire, flood, accident, trespass or other occurrence — excluding hail damage — where the cost of repair to make it roadworthy and legal to operate exceeds the vehicle's retail fair market value immediately before the damage (Colorado DMV, citing C.R.S. 42-6-102(17)(a)(I)).

Two things are unusual here. There is no percentage — the test is repair cost against full pre-damage value, which is a higher bar than the 70–80% thresholds many states use. And that pre-damage value is set by the owner, or by an insurer or other person acting for the owner.

A hail-totalled Colorado car can carry a clean titleIf an insurer writes a car off for hail, the salvage definition does not reach it. The payout happened; the brand did not. Read hail-damaged cars — the mechanical risk is usually low, the cosmetic and resale consequences are not.

The brand, and what it certifies

Colorado's post-repair label is rebuilt from salvage, and the DMV ties it to a specific meaning: the vehicle is now roadworthy as defined in C.R.S. 42-6-102(15). It is a roadworthiness statement, not a quality statement.

Some vehicles are outside the scheme altogether. Collector's items, horseless carriages and street rod vehicles under the relevant article are not salvage vehicles at the time of damage.

Colorado's brand is only part of the record. A history report shows claims and auction entries the title never carried.

Deadlines and dead ends

  • Insurers that acquire a salvage vehicle must apply for a salvage title before transferring ownership (C.R.S. 42-6-136(2)).
  • Owners who keep the car after a claim settlement must apply for a salvage title in their own name.
  • Later purchasers must obtain a salvage title in their name within 60 days, with exceptions for certain non-taxable transactions (C.R.S. 42-6-110).
  • Parts-only sales: a vehicle sold on a bill of sale as parts only cannot be titled in Colorado at all.

That 60-day rule is a practical trap. Buy a Colorado salvage car and the clock is yours, not the seller's.

What the seller must tell you

The seller must disclose the type of damage that caused the salvage designation — excluding hail. Ask for it specifically, because "it was totalled" covers collision, fire and flood equally, and those three lead to very different inspections. If the answer is flood, start with flood-damaged cars.

Buying checklist for Colorado

  1. Ask what caused the damage, by name, and get it in writing.
  2. Assume hail history may not appear on the title. Inspect the roof, bonnet and boot lid in raking light.
  3. If buying salvage, diary the 60-day titling deadline before you pay.
  4. Check for brands applied in other states — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Inspect independently — see the pre-purchase inspection.

Check the full history, including insurance and auction records, before you rely on a clean Colorado title.
