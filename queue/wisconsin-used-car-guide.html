Buying a used car in Wisconsin: the inspection certificate is the document

Last checked November 1, 2026

Wisconsin's rule is short and absolute: a repaired salvage vehicle may not be registered or issued a new certificate of title until it has been examined. The examination produces a certificate, and that certificate is one of four things the application cannot proceed without.

The four required items

An application to retitle a repaired salvage vehicle must include the fees, a properly assigned salvage or dealer certificate of title, any other legally required transfer documents, and an inspection certificate (Wis. Stat. § 342.07). Miss any one of them and the car does not get a title.

That makes the inspection certificate a document that necessarily exists for every legitimately retitled Wisconsin salvage car. Ask for it by name.

What the inspector actually examines

A certified salvage title inspector must inspect repaired vehicles titled as salvage (Wisconsin DOT). The examination covers three distinct things:

  • That the vehicle matches the title it is being retitled against.
  • The source and ownership of the major parts used in the repairs.
  • Compliance with safety equipment requirements.

The governing rule is Wisconsin Administrative Code Trans 149, and the DOT describes the purpose as ensuring the vehicle and its individual parts are not stolen, have proper equipment, and are in safe operating condition.

Wisconsin checks parts ownership, which creates a paper trailBecause the inspector verifies where the major parts came from, the rebuilder had to document it. Those records name donor vehicles — and donor VINs are something you can check yourself.

The inspection covers the rebuild. A history report covers the damage that led to it.

The $80 figure

The applicant pays a fee of $80 to the department for the examination, and if the vehicle passes, the inspector provides an inspection certificate. Like most such fees it is small relative to a rebuild. Treat any explanation that an inspection was skipped for cost reasons as an explanation that does not add up.

What this means when you are standing in front of the car

A Wisconsin salvage car that has been through the process has: a certificate you can read, a parts trail the inspector reviewed, and a safety-equipment check. What it does not have is any assurance about repair quality — whether the structure was pulled back to specification, whether the welds are right, whether the car will behave predictably in another crash.

So the Wisconsin certificate narrows your risk without removing it. Pair it with an independent inspection on a lift — see the pre-purchase inspection — and read whether a rebuilt car is worth it before you settle on a price.

Buying checklist for Wisconsin

  1. Ask for the inspection certificate. It is required, so it exists or the title is irregular.
  2. Ask for the major-parts documentation the inspector reviewed, and check the donor VINs.
  3. Confirm the title you are being shown is the post-inspection title, not the salvage certificate.
  4. Pull the history for brands in other states — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Have the repair quality assessed independently, which no Wisconsin certificate covers.

Check the title and brand history against the VIN before you start evaluating the repair.
