Buying a used car in Montana: rebuilt salvage, and a permit to move it

Last checked November 1, 2026

Montana's rule is compact. One brand, one inspection, one application — and a prohibition with a fine attached that tells you what you may not do with the car before all that is finished.

What goes on the title

After inspection, the department issues a new certificate of title with the words rebuilt salvage printed on it (MCA 61-3-212). There is no graded vocabulary here — no separate flood or fire wording in this section — so a Montana title tells you that the car was salvage and has been rebuilt, and leaves the cause to be established elsewhere.

That makes the supporting documents more important than usual. Ask what the damage actually was.

The application

The owner applies for the new title after the inspection and before operating the vehicle on Montana roads. The application must include:

  • The salvage certificate
  • Receipts or bills of sale for the parts used
  • The inspection report

Those receipts are the part worth chasing. Where used parts came from another vehicle, the bills of sale identify it — and a donor VIN is something you can run yourself.

Ask for the inspection report, not a summary of itThe report is a named document in the application. A seller who has the car legitimately retitled has it, or can get it. A verbal description of what the inspector said is not the same thing.

Donor receipts tell you what went into the car. A history report tells you what happened to it.

The prohibition, and the fine

A person may not operate or use a salvage vehicle on Montana roads except under a temporary registration permit. Failing to comply with this part of the law is a misdemeanour, with a fine of up to $500.

For a buyer this is a practical test of a seller's account. If you are being offered a test drive in a car that is still on a salvage certificate, with no temporary permit, you are being invited into an offence. That tells you something about the transaction before you have looked at a single panel gap.

What the Montana brand does not settle

Because the brand is a single phrase, it does not distinguish a car that was flooded from one that rolled. Those need different inspections entirely — see flood-damaged cars and signs of frame or structural damage.

And a Montana title that carries no brand at all may still belong to a car branded elsewhere. Check the history across states rather than the document in your hand — see title brands and title washing.

Buying checklist for Montana

  1. Confirm which document exists: salvage certificate, or rebuilt salvage title.
  2. Ask for the inspection report and the parts receipts or bills of sale.
  3. Establish the cause of the original loss, which the brand does not state.
  4. Decline a test drive in a car still on a salvage certificate without a permit.
  5. Price it as a branded car — see what a salvage title costs you.

Check the brand history across states before you travel to view a Montana car.
