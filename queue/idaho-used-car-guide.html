Buying a used car in Idaho: brands that come from the national database

Last checked November 1, 2026

Idaho's rules name something most states leave implicit: the national vehicle title database as a direct source of brands. Idaho does not wait for a local event. It reads the national record and brands accordingly.

The brands

BrandWhen it is applied
Rebuilt SalvageA previously salvaged Idaho-titled vehicle that returns to Idaho after being repaired
For Junk OnlyA vehicle the national database reports as scrapped or crushed

The department may also brand a title based on other national-database information that meets the rule's definition of a brand (IDAPA 39.02.05.303).

"For Junk Only" and a car in front of youA vehicle reported scrapped or crushed should not be a car you are being offered to drive. If a national record says scrapped and a seller is showing you a running vehicle, the two facts need reconciling before anything else happens — start with VIN cloning.

Idaho reads the national record to decide what to brand. A history report lets you read it first.

The definitions behind the brands

Idaho's statutory definitions are framed around economics rather than percentages (Idaho Code § 49-123):

  • Salvage vehicle: any vehicle for which a salvage certificate of title, salvage bill of sale or other documentation has been issued — and also one where the owner or insurer finds it uneconomical to repair or rebuild.
  • Rebuilt salvage vehicle: every vehicle previously determined or declared to be a salvage vehicle that has been rebuilt or repaired.
  • Total loss vehicle: every vehicle deemed to be uneconomical to repair.

"Uneconomical to repair" is a judgement, not an arithmetic result. Two similar cars can be treated differently depending on who was assessing and what the car was worth that week.

There is a practical consequence to defining things that way. Where a state uses a percentage, you can reason backwards from the brand to roughly how bad the damage was. In Idaho you cannot. A brand tells you a determination was made, not how severe the event behind it was, so the supporting documents and the physical car carry more of the weight than usual.

The safety standard

Idaho's rule requires a rebuilt vehicle to be in compliance with applicable federal motor vehicle safety standards. That is a compliance obligation rather than a described inspection procedure, so the practical question for a buyer is who verified it and how. Ask.

What this means when shopping

Because Idaho brands from national records, a clean Idaho title has been tested against more than Idaho's own history. That is a genuine positive. But a brand can only be applied from a record that was reported in the first place, and plenty of damage never generates one — see why a clean title does not mean no accidents.

Buying checklist for Idaho

  1. Check for both brands, including For Junk Only.
  2. Ask who determined the car was uneconomical to repair, and when.
  3. For a rebuilt salvage car, ask what documentation supports the safety-standards compliance.
  4. Run a national title history yourself rather than relying on the current document.
  5. Inspect independently — see the pre-purchase inspection.

Check the national title record against the VIN before you take a clean Idaho title at face value.
