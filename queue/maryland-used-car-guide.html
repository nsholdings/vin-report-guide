Buying a used car in Maryland: the embossed salvage certificate

Last checked November 1, 2026

Maryland's rebuilt process produces something unusual: a document with a physical, raised mark on it. If you are buying a rebuilt car in Maryland, that embossing is the most direct evidence you can hold in your hand.

How a Maryland rebuild actually ends

Before a rebuilt title can be issued, the vehicle must pass a Maryland Salvage Inspection, scheduled through the Maryland State Police. The inspection confirms the rebuilding was done properly and that no stolen parts were used, and it is separate from the ordinary safety inspection (Maryland MVA).

At the inspection the applicant presents identification, the salvage certificate, proof of ownership such as a bill of sale or dealer reassignment, repair receipts, and the vehicle itself. The inspector checks the VIN and confirms that no stolen parts were used. If it passes, the inspector embosses and signs the salvage certificate (Maryland State Police). Only then can the owner apply to the MVA for a title and temporary registration.

Ask to see the embossed certificateAn embossed, signed certificate is hard to fake and easy to show. A seller with a genuine Maryland rebuild can produce it in seconds. One who cannot has either lost the central document in the chain or never had it.

The missing-VIN route

There is a separate path worth knowing about. If the VIN plate is missing or damaged, the vehicle must be inspected by the Auto Theft Unit at the Glen Burnie site, on Tuesdays between 8:30 a.m. and 12:30 p.m. The narrowness of that window tells you how exceptional the situation is meant to be.

For a buyer, a car that went down that road is a car whose identity needed to be re-established. That is not automatically sinister — plates get destroyed in fires and collisions — but it is a question you want answered before you buy, not after. See VIN cloning and how it is caught.

Maryland's inspection checks for stolen parts. A history report checks what happened to the car before it got there.

The fee tells you something too

The salvage inspection fee is $75, paid by credit card when scheduling online. That is a modest amount against the cost of a rebuild, which means "it wasn't worth doing" is never a real explanation for a missing inspection. Where the inspection did not happen, the car is not legitimately a rebuilt Maryland vehicle.

What the inspection does not cover

Read the purpose carefully. The Maryland inspection confirms that the rebuild was done and that the parts were not stolen. It is not a judgement that the repair restored the car's crash structure, that panel gaps are right, or that the car will behave normally in a collision. That remains an open question, and it is yours to answer.

Put the car on a lift with an independent mechanic — see the pre-purchase inspection — and read signs of frame or structural damage before you go.

Buying checklist for Maryland

  1. Ask whether the car has ever held a salvage certificate, and get the answer in writing.
  2. If it is rebuilt, ask for the embossed, signed salvage certificate and the repair receipts submitted with it.
  3. Ask whether the VIN plate was ever missing or damaged, which routes the car through the Auto Theft Unit.
  4. Run the VIN for brands applied in other states — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Price it as a branded car. See what a salvage title costs you.

Check the title history across states before you accept a clean Maryland title at face value.
