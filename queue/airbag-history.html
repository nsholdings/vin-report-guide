Airbag deployment history: what to check

Last checked September 20, 2026

Airbags deploy in significant collisions. So a deployment in the car's history tells you two things at once: the car was in a serious crash, and parts of its safety system have been replaced. Both are worth knowing before you buy.

Where deployment shows up

A history report may record whether airbags deployed in a reported accident. Not every accident is reported, and not every report includes that detail — but when it is there, it is one of the most useful lines in the record. See how to read a vehicle history report.

Accident records, including airbag deployment where reported, are in the history record — not in the free lookups.

The physical checks

  • The airbag warning light should come on briefly when you start the car and then go out. A light that stays on means a fault. A light that never comes on at all may have been disabled or its bulb removed — which is a serious warning sign.
  • The steering wheel and dash — a steering wheel or dashboard section that looks newer than the rest of the interior, or doesn't quite match in color or texture
  • Seat belts — pretensioners often fire in the same crash; check that each belt retracts properly
  • Headliner and pillars, where side curtain airbags are fitted — look for sagging or trim that doesn't sit right
Ask who replaced the airbagsReplacement of a deployed airbag system should be done with correct parts by a qualified shop. Ask for the invoice. A seller who can't say where the work was done, or who is vague about it, is leaving you to guess about the system meant to protect you in the next crash.

Recalls

Airbag inflators have been the subject of large safety recalls. Check NHTSA Recalls for open recalls on the VIN, and read what any recall says about driving the car before it is repaired. The remedy is free — see open recalls on used cars.

What it means for the purchase

A deployment isn't an automatic reason to walk away. A car that was properly repaired, with documentation, can be sound. But it is a car that was in a significant crash, and it should be priced and inspected as one. See signs of structural damage.
