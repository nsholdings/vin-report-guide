Deposits and holds: when paying early is safe and when it isn't

Last checked September 20, 2026

"Put down a deposit and I'll hold it for you." Sometimes that is a normal business practice. Sometimes it is the entire scam. The difference is not the request itself — it is what you already know about the seller and the car when it is made.

When a deposit is reasonable

  • You have seen the car in person, or had it independently inspected
  • The VIN you checked matches the car and the title
  • The seller is a verifiable business — a licensed dealer with a physical location
  • The deposit terms, including whether it is refundable, are written down
  • You pay by a method that leaves a record and offers some recourse

When it is a warning sign

  • You have never seen the car and cannot verify the seller
  • The seller wants payment by wire transfer, gift card, cryptocurrency or a payment app between strangers
  • There is time pressure: "other buyers are coming this afternoon"
  • The seller is away — deployed, relocating, overseas — and the car is "in storage" or "with a shipping company"
  • An "escrow" or "buyer protection" service is proposed by the seller rather than chosen by you
The patternAlmost every remote car scam asks for money before the buyer has any way to confirm the car or the seller exist. Irreversible payment methods are chosen because they cannot be reversed.

Before any money moves

  1. Get the VIN and confirm it against a photo of the dashboard plate
  2. Run the free checks — NHTSA Recalls and NICB VINCheck
  3. Pull the full record
  4. Confirm the seller's name matches the title

A deposit on a car with a bad record is money you will spend time trying to recover. Check the record first.

If you do pay a deposit

  • Get a written receipt showing the car's VIN, the amount, the date, what happens to the deposit if the sale does not go ahead, and how long the car is held
  • Pay with a traceable method
  • Keep it small relative to the price

Deposits at dealers

Dealer deposits are common, but terms vary. Ask whether it is refundable before paying, and get the answer in writing. A deposit is not the same as agreeing to every term on a contract you have not yet seen.
