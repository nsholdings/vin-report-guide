Buying a used car in Indiana: REBUILT VEHICLE and the restorer's affidavit

Last checked November 1, 2026

Indiana's rebuilt process is built around a single document that most states do not require by name: the restorer's affidavit. For a buyer, it is the most informative piece of paper attached to the car.

What goes on the title

A rebuilt title in Indiana must conspicuously bear REBUILT VEHICLE, or REBUILT FLOOD DAMAGED VEHICLE where the car was flood damaged (Indiana Code § 9-22-3-15). The flood wording is separated out deliberately — water damage behaves differently from collision damage, and surfaces later. If you see it, read flood-damaged cars before anything else.

The inspection and the affidavit

A rebuilt title is issued only after a state police inspection verifies ownership and the source of the major component parts. Alongside it, the owner must submit a restorer's affidavit listing the component names, their identification numbers and their sources, attached to the salvage title, and surrender that salvage title.

So for any Indiana car carrying the rebuilt brand, a document exists that names every major part and where it came from. Ask for a copy. A rebuilder who did the job properly has no reason to withhold it, and the identification numbers on it can be checked against the car.

What the affidavit is good forMatch the component identification numbers against what is physically on the car. Then cross-check the federal theft-prevention labels on the panels — see the VIN labels on body panels. Two independent records of the same repair should agree.

The affidavit covers the rebuild. A history report covers everything that happened before it.

The exception that removes the brand

There is a carve-out. An insurance company may obtain a title without the rebuilt designation for a recovered stolen vehicle, where the damage did not meet the statute's criteria. The logic is reasonable — a car stolen, recovered and barely touched should not wear a rebuilt brand for the rest of its life.

The consequence for a buyer is that a clean Indiana title is compatible with a theft-and-recovery history. That history matters for other reasons: what happened to the car while it was gone, and whether the recovery was complete. See buying a car that was stolen and recovered.

Penalties, and what they signal

Knowingly or intentionally violating the section is a Class A infraction. The inspecting police officer may charge a fee of up to $5, set by local ordinance. The small fee is a useful tell: an Indiana rebuilt inspection is cheap and routine, so a seller who says it was skipped is not making a cost argument. It was a choice.

Buying checklist for Indiana

  1. Read the exact brand wording — plain rebuilt, or flood damaged.
  2. Ask for the restorer's affidavit and check the component identification numbers against the car.
  3. Ask whether the car was ever a recovered stolen vehicle, since that can carry a clean title.
  4. Pull the title history for records in neighbouring states — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Get it inspected independently — see the pre-purchase inspection.

Check for salvage, theft and flood records against the VIN before you commit.
