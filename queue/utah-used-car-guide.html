Buying a used car in Utah: the form in the windshield

Last checked November 1, 2026

Utah has a disclosure rule you can use without asking a single question. Dealers must display the branded-title notice in a specific corner of the windshield, which means on a Utah lot you can check a car's status while you are still walking towards it.

Where to look

Before negotiating a sale of a salvage or branded vehicle, a dealer must give the customer and any prospective lienholder written notice that a salvage certificate or branded title has been issued — and must display that form prominently in the lower passenger-side corner of the windshield (Utah DMV, citing Utah Code § 41-1a-1004).

Non-dealers must use the same disclosure form but are not required to display it in the windshield. So on a private sale you have to ask — and you should.

Also check at auctionA motor vehicle auction operator must give a purchaser of a salvage vehicle a disclosure statement with wording set by statute (Utah Code § 41-3-201). If you are buying through an auction, that statement should exist. See buying auction cars.

No percentage, and three brands

Utah does not publish a percentage. A salvage vehicle is one damaged by collision, flood or another occurrence where the cost of repairing it for safe operation exceeds its fair market value. The definition also covers a vehicle an insurer or another jurisdiction has declared salvage but which is not barred from registration.

Brands do not come off

Utah is explicit: brands cannot be removed — not for repaired vehicles, not for flood vehicles, and not for vehicles branded by another state. A Utah title that is clean today is a title that has not been branded anywhere Utah could see.

The lemon-law disclosure repeats

For a manufacturer buyback, the manufacturer or any later seller must disclose in writing, clearly and conspicuously, that the vehicle is a manufacturer buyback. The disclosure must be included in each sale or lease contract, and must be provided every time the vehicle is resold.

That obligation does not expire after the first resale, which is unusual and useful. If you are looking at a Utah car with a buyback brand, the disclosure should be in your contract. See manufacturer buyback titles.

Buying checklist for Utah

  1. On a dealer lot, read the lower passenger-side corner of the windshield first.
  2. From a private seller, ask for the same disclosure form in writing.
  3. Check whether the contract carries a manufacturer buyback disclosure.
  4. Pull a multi-state history — see title brands and title washing.
  5. Inspect independently — see the pre-purchase inspection.

Check for salvage, flood and buyback records against the VIN before you visit the lot.
