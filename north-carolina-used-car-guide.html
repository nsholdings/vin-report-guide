Buying a used car in North Carolina: the damage disclosure statement and title brands

Last checked October 4, 2026

North Carolina gives buyers a written statement from the seller about damage, salvage, flood, theft and reconstruction, plus a set of title brands and a physical marker for insurer total losses. Used together, they make it harder to hide a car's history — if you read them.

The Damage Disclosure Statement (MVR-181)

The NCDMV's Damage Disclosure Statement, form MVR-181, states that state law requires every seller to disclose certain information to the buyer. It asks five questions:

  1. Has the vehicle been damaged by collision or other occurrence to the extent that damages exceed 25% of its value at the time?
  2. Was the vehicle a salvage motor vehicle?
  3. Is it a flood vehicle?
  4. Is it a recovered theft vehicle?
  5. Has it been reconstructed?

Questions 1 and 4 apply only to vehicles five model years old and newer. The form explains how to count: the vehicle's model year is the first model year and the current calendar year is the last. In 2026, that means model years 2022 through 2026.

The underlying statute, G.S. 20-71.4, also makes it an offense to transfer a vehicle when the seller knows it is or was a flood, reconstructed or salvage vehicle without disclosing that in writing — and that part applies regardless of age.

On an older car, the 25% damage question doesn't applyFor a vehicle more than five model years old, a "no" on the form does not cover collision damage. Run a history check and inspect the car for repairs — see signs of frame damage.

North Carolina title brands

North Carolina's title-branding statute, G.S. 20-71.3, lists the brands: Salvage Motor Vehicle, Salvage Rebuilt Vehicle, Reconstructed Vehicle, Flood Vehicle and Non-U.S.A. Vehicle. Once a title is branded, later titles must continue to show it.

The NCDMV explains the main terms on its special-cases page. A salvaged vehicle is one damaged to the extent that repairs to make it safe for public roads would exceed 75 percent of its fair market value. A flood vehicle is one submerged or partly submerged to the point of damaging the body, engine, transmission or differential. The same page says vehicles six model years old or newer need anti-theft inspections by the State Highway Patrol before retitling.

The statute describes those inspections as anti-theft measures that do not certify safety or road-worthiness. A rebuilt North Carolina car still needs your own pre-purchase inspection.

North Carolina title brands carry into national title data. Check the VIN against what the seller wrote on the disclosure.

Look for the doorjamb marker

Under G.S. 20-71.3, when an insurer declares a vehicle a total loss, the title and registration carry the words "TOTAL LOSS CLAIM", and a permanent tamperproof marker is placed in the doorjamb. Open the doors and look. If there is a marker, or signs that one has been removed, compare that with what the title and disclosure say.

Inspections after you buy

The NCDMV says a vehicle registered in North Carolina must pass an annual safety inspection before its registration can be renewed; vehicles more than 30 years old are exempt. Emissions inspections apply in 19 counties: Alamance, Buncombe, Cabarrus, Cumberland, Davidson, Durham, Forsyth, Franklin, Gaston, Guilford, Iredell, Johnston, Lincoln, Mecklenburg, New Hanover, Randolph, Rowan, Union and Wake.

Odometer disclosure has limits

North Carolina's odometer statute, G.S. 20-347, exempts a vehicle that is model year 2010 or older from the disclosure requirement, among other exemptions. On those cars, there may be no formal mileage statement at all, so check service records and history reports for consistency. See odometer rollback.

North Carolina checklist

  1. Get a completed MVR-181 and read all five answers.
  2. On cars over five model years old, don't rely on the 25% damage answer.
  3. Read the title for any of the five brands.
  4. Check the doorjamb for a total-loss marker.
  5. Run a VIN check and get an independent inspection.
